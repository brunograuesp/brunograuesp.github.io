About Me

Engineer on the page, athlete on the board.

I’m a Mechanical Engineering student at the University of Wyoming and a diver for the Cowboys, and I grew up in Barcelona. Walking that city every day, you see engineering, architecture, and art built into the same wall, and it taught me early that a building is never only structure: it shapes the everyday life of everyone who passes through it. That idea still drives me. Engineering done well, with some art in it, quietly makes ordinary days better.

Diving made me analytical. It is a sport of small details, where a few degrees of rotation or the angle of a hand decides the result, so I learned to really see and understand the little things. I carry that same attention into building ideas and projects. More than a decade in high performance sport, all of it alongside a full course load, also made me disciplined about showing up and doing the unglamorous work.

The engineering I care about runs from analysis to hardware: sizing a problem by hand, checking it against a SolidWorks or thermal model, then building the thing to see whether the theory holds. I use AI assisted workflows to move faster through the repetitive parts. Two internships in Barcelona, one in engineering and one in business design, taught me to tie a technical solution back to the real problem it solves for a client.

I have worked in cross cultural teams across internships and coursework, and most of all in my day to day as a Division I student athlete, where teammates and staff come from all over the world. It has made me open minded and genuinely welcoming to different cultures and ideas, in leadership, in work, and in how a team is built.

Away from the lab and the pool I am usually outside: snowboarding, hiking, surfing, or on the beach, and always following FC Barcelona. Art stays close too. I help run Myriam Espinosa Art, my mum’s practice, and I still get pulled toward any city worth looking up in.

Track Following Autonomous Mobile Platform

University of Wyoming · Spring 2026

Composite Wall Heat Transfer Analysis

Calculated 1D heat transfer through a ten section composite wall using thermal circuit analysis and identified an alternative insulation material that reduced heat loss by 52%. Validated the result against a 2D SolidWorks thermal simulation, reconciling a 20 to 28% deviation by isolating multidimensional heat flow effects the 1D model misses. Projected about $200 in annual heating savings per structure, backed by an economic and environmental impact assessment.

  • Thermal circuit analysis
  • SolidWorks Simulation
  • MATLAB
  • Cost / impact modeling

More coursework projects

Machine Design · Spring 2026

Automated Bearing Selection

A MATLAB tool that sizes 02 series deep groove ball bearings to a target reliability and design life. Given radial and axial load, speed, and required life, it iterates on the bearing X/Y factors, computes the equivalent load and required dynamic rating, then steps through catalog bore sizes until a bearing clears the requirement, then verifies the achieved reliability. Tested across five load cases, including a high reliability (R = 0.999) and an extended life (50,000 hr) scenario.

  • MATLAB
  • Iterative solver
  • Machine design
  • Reliability / L10 life

Fluid Mechanics · Spring 2026

Pipe Flow Loss Characterization

Instrumented a benchtop water loop with pressure taps and an orifice plate to measure how much energy a piping system loses to pipe friction and to fittings like elbows and tees. Compared the results against textbook values and traced the gap to pipe age and internal roughness, with measured friction running about a third above the smooth pipe ideal.

  • Internal flow
  • Flow measurement
  • Instrumentation
  • Data analysis
